# Master the HR & Behavioral Round

## Objective
The HR/behavioral round decides who converts a technical "yes" into an offer. It tests **soft skills, attitude, and fit** — and it's entirely preparable. Most students lose here by being unprepared, vague, or nervous.

## Steps
1. **Prepare answers to the top 20 HR questions.** The classic set:
   - "Tell me about yourself" (see `year3-prepare-self-introduction`)
   - Why this company? Why this role?
   - What are your strengths and weaknesses?
   - Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
   - Why should we hire you over other candidates?
   - Any questions for us? (Always have 2–3 — see `year3-research-companies-before-interviews`)
2. **Use the STAR method** for behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time you..."). Situation → Task → Action → Result. This is the #1 formula interviewers want.
3. **Prepare 5–6 STAR stories** from your college life: a project with a deadline crunch, a team conflict, a failure, a leadership moment, helping someone, a time you went beyond requirements.
4. **Know your resume cold.** HR will pick one line from it and dig. Rehearse your projects and internships (see `year3-explain-projects-in-interviews`).
5. **Practice body language:** sit straight, smile, make eye contact, speak at a measured pace, avoid filler words ("um", "like").
6. **Know the company + the offer context** before the round — see `year3-research-companies-before-interviews`.

## Proof
- Written, rehearsed answers to all top-20 HR questions.
- Five STAR-format stories you can tell in under 2 minutes each.
- Two successful mock HR interviews with a friend or senior.

## Mentor Note
HR rounds aren't about trick questions — they're about **honesty, confidence, and structure**. Rehearsal converts panic into calm. Never exaggerate your resume (they cross-check), always have questions ready, and remember: by this round the company mostly wants to say yes — don't give them a reason not to.
