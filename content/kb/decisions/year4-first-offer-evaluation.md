# Should I Accept the First Offer?

## Short Answer
**Almost always yes — with two caveats.** An offer in hand is worth a lot of un-cracked dreams. Accept it, keep it safe, and only switch if a genuinely better offer arrives *with a written commitment*. See `year4-service-vs-product-company` for the type-level trade-offs.

## Accept if:
- It's your first/only offer. Take it — placements are competitive and the next round is never guaranteed.
- The compensation, role, and location meet your minimum bar.
- You have a job-hunting plan B only if this offer expires.

## Consider carefully before accepting if:
- You have a **confirmed, higher-value offer** (product company) with a written letter. Then pick the better one.
- The offer is from a company you'd strongly regret joining (very low pay, no learning). But even then — a "poor" offer beats "no offer" unless you have a solid plan B.

## Key Rules
1. **Read the offer letter** before deciding: CTC breakdown, bond/notice period (TCS/Infosys often have 1–2 year bonds), joining location, probation, stipend vs full pay, and exit clauses.
2. **Never rely on verbal promises** — get everything in writing.
3. **Check the joining window** — some offers require you to join within months; others have a long lead. This affects GATE/MS timing (see `year4-job-vs-gate-vs-ms`).
4. **Don't hoard offers** — if you hold multiple, release the ones you won't take; it's both ethical and keeps your college's placement process fair.

## Mentor Recommendation
The first offer is your **safety net**, not your forever. Accept it, then continue to grow — apply to better companies for the next intake, do GATE if it fits, or keep interviewing. You can resign per the notice period. The worst financial decision is refusing a real offer betting on a hoped-for one.
