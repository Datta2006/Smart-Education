# How to Explain Projects in Interviews

## Trigger condition
The student has projects on their resume but freezes or rambles when asked to explain them, or their descriptions feel weak.

## Mentor message
Every project explanation should follow the **Problem → Approach → Impact** structure. Rehearse it until it's 60–90 seconds:

1. **Problem (2 sentences):** What real problem did you solve? Why does it matter?
2. **Approach (3–4 sentences):** What did you build, and how? What was the tech stack, and **why** did you choose it? Mention challenges you faced and how you overcame them — this is what interviewers probe for.
3. **Impact (1–2 sentences):** Result in numbers — "serves 200 students", "reduces lookup time by 40%", "500+ lines of optimized SQL". Metrics make it credible.
4. **Future work (1 sentence):** What would you do next? It signals ownership and vision.

**Common traps to avoid:**
- Reciting code you can't explain — interviewers may ask you to write blocks of your project code, so **revisit your code** before interviews.
- Vague language ("a cool app that does stuff") — replace with specifics.
- Going too long — trim to the essentials; let the interviewer pull out details.

Rehearse out loud with a friend or recording 3–5 times per project. The STAR-style structure works for internships too. See `year3-research-companies-before-interviews`.
