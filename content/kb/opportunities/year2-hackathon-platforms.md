# Hackathons (Smart India, Unstop, Devpost, MLH)

## Objective
Hackathons compress a semester of learning into a weekend: you build a product end-to-end, work in a team, present to judges, and leave with projects + connections for your resume. Year 2 is the ideal year to start winning.

## Opportunities
1. **Smart India Hackathon (SIH)** — India's flagship national hackathon, run by the Ministry of Education. Teams of 6 solve real government/industry problem statements; ₹1 lakh+ prizes and internship opportunities. Register through your college's SPOC each year (typically Sep–Nov). → https://www.sih.gov.in/
2. **Unstop** — India's largest platform for hackathons, competitions, and scholarships. One profile gets you into dozens of campus + national events. → https://unstop.com/
3. **Devpost** — Global hackathon directory (MLH-powered). Browse 100s of online + in-person hackathons with prizes. → https://devpost.com/
4. **Hack2Skill** — Indian hackathon aggregator with good college-level events. → https://hack2skill.com/
5. **MLH Hackathons** — The biggest network of university hackathons globally (many online). → https://mlh.io/
6. **HackerEarth Hackathons** — Company-run coding + hackathon challenges. → https://www.hackerearth.com/challenges/hackathon/

## Action Steps
- Aim for **2–3 hackathons per year**, prioritizing one "big" one (SIH or an MLH event).
- Go with a team of 2–4 with mixed skills (one strong coder, one designer, one presenter).
- Reuse your best projects — a strong portfolio piece built at a hackathon is interview material.
- After each one, write a 5-line reflection: what you built, what broke, what you'd do differently.

## Mentor note
Judges and recruiters care about **finished, working demos**, not complexity. A polished simple app that works beats a broken ambitious one. Hackathons also teach you the soft skills — pitching, teamwork, working under pressure — that no course covers.
