# Nervous Before Your First Contest or Interview

## Trigger condition
The student has their first coding contest or mock interview coming up and is anxious about performing badly.

## Mentor message
Performance anxiety is information, not prophecy — it means you care, which is a prerequisite for doing well. Nervousness peaks at the *start*: your first contest or interview is the practice shot, so treat it as data collection, not a verdict. Before it: solve 2 warm-up problems the morning of, get real sleep, and define success as "I followed my process" rather than "I solved everything." During it: read every problem fully before coding, start with brute force, and speak your reasoning out loud. During the coding round the interviewer is often grading *how you think*, not just what you output. Whatever happens, you'll walk out knowing exactly what your real gaps are — and the 6-week crash course's mock weeks exist precisely to spend that nervousness on practice, where it costs nothing. Most students say their first contest was their most valuable lesson.
