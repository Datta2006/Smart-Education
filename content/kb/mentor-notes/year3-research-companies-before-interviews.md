# Research Companies Before You Interview

## Trigger condition
The student has an upcoming interview but hasn't looked into the company, its products, or its hiring process.

## Mentor message
Interviewers notice immediately when a candidate knows nothing about the company. Before every interview, spend 2–3 hours researching:

1. **Products/services** — what the company actually builds and sells.
2. **Key people** — founders, CEO, CTO (their backgrounds often hint at what the company values).
3. **Competitors** — who they compete with and why they win/lose.
4. **Tech stack** — check their engineering blog or job postings for the languages/frameworks they use.
5. **The role** — read the JD twice and map your resume to each requirement.
6. **Recent news** — funding, product launches, hiring sprees.

Then prepare **2–3 meaningful questions** for the interviewer (never leave an interview without asking questions). Good examples: "What does the team's typical week look like?", "What challenges is the team facing?", "How is success measured in this role?"

This research converts you from "another candidate" to "someone who actually wants to work here" — and it makes you more confident in every round. See also `year3-explain-projects-in-interviews`.
