# How to Find a Mentor (Cold Outreach That Works)

## Trigger condition
The student wants a mentor (senior, alumni, or professional) but doesn't know how to approach one, or fears being ignored.

## Mentor message
A mentor doesn't fall from the sky — you build the relationship with a **specific, low-effort ask**. Here's the exact playbook:

1. **Start with your own college first.** Seniors in Year 3/4, alumni placed at companies, professors who run labs — they're one DM away and usually happy to help their juniors. Join your college's placement/tech WhatsApp/Telegram groups.
2. **Find people one step ahead of you.** A Year 3 senior who just got an internship is more approachable and more useful than a VP at Google. Same for alumni working at companies you target.
3. **Cold outreach format that gets replies** (LinkedIn/email/DM): one sentence on who you are → one specific line proving you know their work (their project, their company, their post) → ONE small ask (e.g., "can I ask 3 questions about your interview prep?" or "would you review my resume?"). No "please be my mentor" — that's too big an ask.
4. **Never ask for a job in the first message.** Mentorship comes first; opportunities follow naturally.
5. **Respect their time:** come prepared with specific questions, act on their advice, and follow up with a thank-you when it works.

A mentor is a force-multiplier: they compress years of trial-and-error into a few conversations. One good senior relationship in Year 2 will pay dividends through Year 4 — including referrals at hiring time.
