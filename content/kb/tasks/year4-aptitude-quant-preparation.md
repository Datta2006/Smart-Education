# Crack Aptitude & Quantitative Rounds

## Objective
Almost every IT/PSU company runs an **aptitude + reasoning + English** round before the technical screen. These are *very learnable* and often the cheapest marks you'll earn — most students fail them only because they never practice.

## Steps
1. **Know the syllabus.** It's a standard set: quantitative aptitude (percentages, ratios, time & work, profit & loss, probability, averages, simple/compound interest), logical reasoning (series, syllogisms, seating arrangements, coding-decoding, blood relations), verbal ability (synonyms, antonyms, sentence correction, reading comprehension), plus current affairs for some PSUs.
2. **Learn the formulas once** — 1 week of theory from a standard source (IndiaBix or any aptitude book) is enough.
3. **Practice daily for 30–45 minutes** for 2–3 months. Speed is the real skill — most people run out of time, not ability.
4. **Use mock tests** on the actual platform the company uses (TCS NQT uses its own interface; HackerEarth/AMCAT-style for others) so the UI doesn't surprise you.
5. **Master time management:** attempt easy questions first, don't get stuck on one problem, manage the sectional time limit.

## Proof
- Score above 90th percentile on 3 consecutive IndiaBix/online mock tests.
- Completed at least one full mock test on the target company's platform (TCS NQT, AMCAT, etc.).
- You can answer any aptitude question type in under a minute.

## Mentor Note
Aptitude rounds feel trivial to engineers — until they fail one. But they're a **huge, low-effort differentiator**: 30 minutes a day for 3 months converts a 40% pass rate into a 90% one. Treat it as a morning habit, not a cram, and it'll never cost you a job.
