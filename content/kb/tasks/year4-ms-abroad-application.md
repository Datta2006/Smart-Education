# MS Abroad — Application Timeline & Process

## Objective
Applying for an MS (US, Germany, Canada, Australia, etc.) is a **12–18 month process** that overlaps with Year 4. This card gives you the full timeline so nothing surprises you. (If you're unsure whether MS is right for you, see `year4-job-vs-gate-vs-ms` first.)

## The Timeline (apply in Year 4, start the fall after or the next year)
1. **Year 3 → Year 4 summer:** Take the GRE (if US/Canada; most German/European programs don't need it) and IELTS/TOEFL. Book 2–3 months ahead. GRE prep: [GregMat](https://www.gregmat.com/) or [Manhattan](https://www.manhattanprep.com/gre/).
2. **Month 2–4 (Aug–Oct):** Shortlist 6–10 universities by program, tuition, scholarships, and placement outcomes. Finalize your **Letter of Recommendation (LOR)** writers — professors who know your project/CGPA well. Ask early, with details.
3. **Month 4–6 (Oct–Dec):** Write your **Statement of Purpose (SOP)** — 2+ weeks of drafts. It must answer: Why this program? Why you? What will you contribute? Your story should connect your projects (see `year4-final-year-project`) to your goals. Get it reviewed (admissions consultants or seniors).
4. **December–January:** Submit applications (most US deadlines Nov 15 – Jan 15). Keep a spreadsheet: school, deadline, status, documents.
5. **January–April:** Interviews (some programs), acceptances, and **scholarship/funding** decisions. German public universities often have near-zero tuition but need [APS](https://www.apsindia.org/) certification — start that early.
6. **April–June:** Confirm, apply for student visa (US F-1, German student visa), arrange funding evidence.

## Proof
- GRE + IELTS/TOEFL scores by December.
- 6–10 applications submitted with SOP, LORs, and CV.
- At least 1 admission offer or clear funding path by April.

## Mentor Note
The SOP is the soul of your application — spend real weeks on it. Your CGPA and GRE get you past the filter; your SOP and LORs get you the offer. Also, funding (scholarships like [Fulbright](https://us.fulbrightonline.org/), Germany's DAAD) changes everything, so research it *before* applying, not after. See `year4-ms-scholarships-abroad`.
