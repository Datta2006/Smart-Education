# Scholarships & Funding for MS Abroad

## Objective
An MS abroad can cost ₹20–60 lakh (US) or near-zero tuition (Germany/Nordic). Funding changes everything — research it *before* applying, not after. This card lists the major sources.

## Types of Funding

### 1. Government / National Scholarships
- **Fulbright** (US) — full funding for Indian students, extremely competitive → https://us.fulbrightonline.org/
- **DAAD** (Germany) — scholarships + fully-funded positions → https://www.daad.de/en/
- **Chevening** (UK) — fully funded, one-year Master's → https://www.chevening.org/
- **Commonwealth Scholarship** (UK) → https://cscuk.fcdo.gov.uk/

### 2. University-Based Funding
- Most US universities offer **assistantships (TA/RA)** that cover tuition + stipend, and merit scholarships. Apply early and mention funding needs in essays.
- German public universities have **near-zero tuition**; living costs (~₹8–10 lakh/yr) can be covered by part-time work (20 hrs/week).
- Nordic countries (Sweden, Norway, Finland) offer tuition scholarships like the **Swedish Institute Scholarship** → https://si.se/en/

### 3. Private / Corporate
- Indian banks offer **education loans** (SBI Global Ed-Vantage, etc.) — plan repayment against expected US/European salaries.
- Company sponsorships (rare for freshers) — apply after 1–2 years of work instead.

## Key Steps
- Build a **funding strategy** before finalizing universities: target schools with assistantships/scholarships you qualify for.
- Include funded options in your shortlist (see `year4-ms-abroad-application`).
- Apply to 1–2 scholarships even if you think you won't get them — they're learnable.

## Mentor Note
"MS is too expensive" is often a *funding gap*, not a real barrier. Germany and Nordic programs can be nearly free, and TA/RA assistantships can cover a US MS. The difference between funded and unfunded is a few months of careful research — do it before you apply, so you never self-reject on cost.
