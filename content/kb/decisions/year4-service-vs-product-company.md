# Service Company vs Product Company — First Job

## Short Answer
**Take what you can get, but know the trade-off.** A **service job** (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture) is the reliable, common first step; a **product job** (Amazon, Flipkart, startups) pays more and grows faster but is harder to land. Both are valid — the choice is about your goals and risk appetite.

## Choose a Service Company if:
- You need a job **now** and your profile is still being built.
- The mass-recruiter offer is your only one — take it; a job beats no job.
- You want structure, training, and a safe start (most graduates in India start here).

**Reality check:** service roles involve more support/maintenance work early, slower pay growth, and less cutting-edge tech. But you get experience, a salary, and often internal mobility — and you can switch to product companies later (many do).

## Choose a Product Company if:
- You get the offer and it's a real engineering role.
- You prioritize growth, learning, compensation, and brand.
- Your DSA + project profile is strong enough to crack them.

**Reality check:** product companies are competitive, with high expectations and usually faster pace. But the long-term growth and pay difference is significant.

## The Hybrid Strategy
- **Apply to both** — mass recruiters as safety, product companies as targets.
- If you land a product offer, take it. If you land only a service offer, **take it and keep interviewing**; switch within 1–2 years. Referrals (see `year2-find-a-mentor`) make the switch far easier.
- Never stay in a role where you've stopped learning for more than a year — growth beats title.

## Mentor Recommendation
First job = **opportunity floor, not destiny.** The 3–5 year plan matters more than year one. Optimize for "learning + reputation + switchability" regardless of which type you start with. See `year3-offcampus-fresher-companies` for the full target list.
