# Mock Interviews — Your Secret Weapon

## Trigger condition
The student is preparing for placements but has never done a single mock interview, or avoids them out of nervousness.

## Mentor message
The gap between "I know DSA" and "I can pass an interview" is **practice speaking while solving**. Mock interviews close that gap. Here's how to run them cheaply:

1. **Find partners:** 2–3 classmates also prepping, your college placement group, or online (Pramp, meetups). → https://www.pramp.com/
2. **Run them realistically:** 45 minutes, one problem at a time, *think out loud*, no silent coding. Time-box each phase.
3. **Rotate roles:** be interviewer sometimes — you'll learn what interviewers look for.
4. **Record and review:** watch yourself; note filler words, long silences, and missed clarifications.
5. **Get feedback on:**
   - Did you clarify constraints before coding?
   - Did you explain approach + complexity?
   - Did you communicate, or just code?
6. **Progress:** start with easy problems, work up to mediums and hards under a timer.

Start mock interviews **2–3 months before** your first real interview. The first one will feel awkward — everyone's does. By mock #5 you'll be calm, and by real interview day it's just another practice round. See also `interview-nerves-first-contest` and `year3-explain-projects-in-interviews`.
