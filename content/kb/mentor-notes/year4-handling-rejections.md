# Handling Rejections & Placement Pressure

## Trigger condition
The student failed an online assessment, interview, or placement round — or saw their peers getting offers while they haven't — and is demoralized or anxious.

## Mentor message
Rejection in placements is **normal, common, and not a verdict on you.** Here's how to process it and keep moving:

1. **Feel it, then time-box it.** It's fine to be upset for a day. Then get back to work — the next company's assessment doesn't care about last week's rejection.
2. **Diagnose, don't dramatize.** Was it aptitude speed, a DSA pattern, an HR answer, or a bad day? That's data, not destiny. Fix the specific gap and retry (see `year4-aptitude-quant-preparation`, `year4-hr-behavioral-rounds`).
3. **Rejection rates are brutal at every level.** Even strong candidates get rejected 5–10 times before an offer. Companies reject based on fit and competition, not just skill.
4. **Keep a rejection log** with a one-line reason + one-line improvement. Watching "no" become "interview" become "offer" is real proof of progress.
5. **You are not your result.** Your value isn't a company's decision. Peers getting offers is not a judgment of you — see `year2-comparison-trap` and `placement-prep-behind-peers`.
6. **Keep options open.** Rejection at company A doesn't end the season — see `year4-job-vs-gate-vs-ms` for backup tracks.

**Concrete next step:** pick the *next* company from your shortlist (see `year3-apply-offcampus-companies`), set a 1-week improvement goal, and apply today. Momentum beats morale.
